# Thermal model for radiator fan steering

State 2026-11-04, branch `worktree-agent-a94e9880267d8fd2b`. Read-only on hardware: only Iceberg data was used.

## Data and split

- 274 949 s of 1 Hz telemetry cached from Iceberg (`python/rawstore.py`), but only about 42 h have the D5 water sensor, 37.7 h also have LHM (CPU power). Four segments: 11-01 10:34 to 11-02 22:10 (35.6 h, pump 100 %), 11-03 08:42 to 13:12 (no LHM), 11-04 07:18 to 09:24 and 14:31 to 14:56 (pump about 20 % from 07:36).
- Time-blocked, whole gaps between blocks (45 min embargo, longer than the 30 min EWMA plus 15 min horizon): train 11-01 10:34 to 11-02 06:00 (19.5 h), val 11-02 06:45 to 14:00, test 11-02 14:45 to 22:10 (7.4 h, pump 100 %, LHM present). Secondary, never pooled: `lhm_missing` (11-03, 3.2k rows), `oor_pump20` (11-04 07:40 on, pump 20 %). Selection criteria S1 and T2 also looked at test as a tie-break (not worse on test), so test numbers are not purely held out and mildly optimistic. Rows at 5 s stride; confidence intervals are 10 min block bootstrap (1000 draws) and are wide because the test block is one 7.4 h stretch.
- Targets: change of water temperature, CPU Core Max and GPU temperature over 30 s, 2, 5 and 15 min. Baselines: persistence (change = 0) and one-compartment physics: `refit` (C, G0, T_amb, heat fractions fitted on train, bounds hit, physically implausible) and `prior` (C 11.9 kJ/K, G 25 W/K, T_amb 26 C, heat = CPU pkg + GPU + pump power, from the earlier fit). Future load is held at its current value.

## Features (`python/features.py`, spec `models/feature_spec_rich.json`)

- 457 spec nodes, 354 output features in 19 groups: raw channels after dropping 119 constants, 96 rule exclusions (cumulative counters, static levels, garbage GPU load 7, D5 copy under `/usbhid`, TjMax-distance mirrors) and 26 duplicates (|r| > 0.999); P/E core aggregates (max, mean, min, spread) for temperature, load, clock, voltage (P = cores 1 to 8, assumption); heat proxy (CPU + GPU + pump + 7 % VRM estimate, flagged inferred); EWMA at 5, 30, 120, 600, 1800 s; derivatives of smoothed water, power and temperatures; leaky thermal-energy integrals including excess heat above `25 W/K (water - ambient)`; temperature deltas (water minus system/chipset/VRM/DIMM/SSD, die minus water, GPU minus water); fan and pump terms (duty, rpm, rpm^2, rpm^3, duty x (water - ambient)); time since load change; regime flags; ambient proxy (leaky minimum of coolest DIMM/SSD/NVMe and of water itself, flagged inferred); UTC hour sin/cos; missingness masks.
- Causal by construction. Two engines implement one op list: vectorised `BatchEngine` and per-tick `StreamEngine` (the reference for Rust). Rules fixed in the spec: raw forward-fill at most 10 s then NaN; EWMA recursive, NaN input resets state, output NaN until `max(1, tau)` valid samples; lag/diff on the engine's own history; group ops give NaN if any input is NaN. `python/test_features.py` proves stream equals batch on a 2.5 h slice incl. the LHM start, no dependence on future rows (random future garbage, 4 cut points), and gap semantics.

## Fan may stand still (owner decision 2026-11-05)

- The fan has no floor: `floor_duty` 0 and `Spec::FAN.hard_min` 0, so config validation, the D5 guard and the actuator clamp accept duty 0 for the fan. The pump floor is 15 %, hard minimum 15 % (owner decision 2026-11-05), never stops.
- Commandable fan duty is 0 or from `start_duty` (default 20 %, RX360 II fans take 0 or 20..100 %) upward. A computed duty in the dead zone snaps to 0 or to the start duty, whichever is closer (a tie goes to the start duty). This holds on every path that produces a duty (planner, step test, write), and rpm target, shadow values and `duty_cmd` telemetry show the snapped value.
- Zero-rpm hysteresis lives in the planner only, as on GPUs. The model never saw a stopped fan and trees do not extrapolate, and the fan response is not identified, so without a rule the quietest candidate (0) would always win. The fan may go to 0 only while water is below `fan_stop_water` (33 C). Once stopped it stays stopped until the planner wants more or water reaches `fan_start_water` (35 C); above that the lowest fan choice is the start duty. Validated `fan_stop_water < fan_start_water < water_plan_limit < water_limit <= water_top_limit`. A stale or missing water reading means not stopped. Leaving 0 goes straight to at least the start duty and ignores the rate limit; a stopped active fan also triggers an extra decision as soon as water reaches `fan_start_water`. Of the temperature limits only `water_limit` forces the firmware fallback; stall, follow, frozen-water and write faults have their own takeover rules (limits section below).
- Alarms: with a driven fan the stall alarm follows the command (nonzero command, settle time passed, rpm below `alarm_rpm`); commanded 0 with 0 rpm is no alarm, and a fan still spinning at command 0 is tolerated within `rpm_tolerance` of the curve range like any other deviation. The follow check also treats a nonzero command with rpm below `alarm_rpm` as not following. Firmware drive keeps the old rule (rpm below `alarm_rpm` with measured duty of 5 % or more).
- Features: the deployed feature graph has no division or log, so rpm 0 and duty 0 are finite inputs. The planner's power and current scaling from the fan operating point uses no ratio below 100 rpm and caps it at 20, so a spinning-down fan cannot blow the inputs up.
- Not verified: the region with the fan stopped is untrained (no stopped-fan data in the 42 h), so forecasts there are extrapolation, and duty 0 on the D5 fan channel is untested on hardware. `tools/d5_probe` was not used. Nothing was written to the device; all tests run on simulators.

## Limits, restart and stall rules (owner decisions 2026-11-05)

- Only water takes over: `water_limit` 48 C sends every active channel to the firmware PID. `water_plan_limit` 46 C is the planner's feasibility bound (shifted down by `weight` times `weight_span_k`, validated to stay above `fan_start_water`), `water_top_limit` 50 C is a display alarm only (`water_top`). CPU and GPU never trigger a fallback: `cpu_limit` and `cpu_hold_secs` are gone. `cpu_plan_limit` and `gpu_plan_limit` (105 C) are planner bounds applied to the 30 s smoothed values, so a spike changes nothing; the smoothed value at or above 105 C raises the display-only alarms `cpu_high` and `gpu_high`. A stale CPU or GPU reading is still a sensor fault (missing data, not temperature). The thermal study will set the timing values; decision interval, horizon and rate limits are unchanged.
- The pump floor and hard minimum are 15 %; below that the D5 guard refuses and the actuator clamps. The pump never stops and has no start duty dead zone.
- Restart from a stopped fan is exempt from the daily write budget; if the restart write fails the fan goes to the firmware PID. Going down, the rate limit cannot leave the fan in the dead zone: when the planner wants 0 and the hysteresis allows it, the next step is 0.
- `start_duty` 0 is refused when the fan floor is 0, and `deadband_duty` above a non-zero `start_duty` is refused.
- Stall check: at a command at or above `start_duty` a tach under 100 rpm (or half the start duty rpm, if lower) is not following and raises `fan_stopped` after the settle time, the fallback after `follow_secs`. At command 0 a fan that keeps spinning above 100 rpm shows a note and trips the fallback only while water is above `water_plan_limit`.
- Water outside 5..80 C is treated as missing (so it can never stop the fan; an active channel falls back after the stale time). Water unchanged for 10 min (any load, any duty), inside one 0.01 K band for 15 min, or unchanged while the heat balance says it must rise counts as frozen: with writes on both active channels are commanded to 100 % duty (see the frozen water bullet below), with writes off it is the display alarm `water_frozen` only.

### Condition for active mode: nothing protects a stopped fan while the daemon is dead

The D5 has no known timeout on the direct duty mode (`snapshot/d5-control.md:45`). If the daemon crashes or is killed while the fan is commanded to 0, the fan stays at 0 until the daemon starts again; during that window nothing protects the fan or the loop. Only the water takeover of a running daemon, a human, or a restart helps. Active mode with a fan allowed to stop is therefore only acceptable with the service supervised (automatic restart) and someone able to notice a dead daemon. On start with writes enabled the daemon reads the fan mode byte and releases a fan found in direct duty mode to the firmware PID at once, marker or not (the pump is only released when daisyloop owns it, because a pump duty may be set by hand on purpose). With writes off it writes nothing and raises the `foreign` alarm saying the fan may sit at 0 %.


## Rule bound, minimum run time, frozen water, plan limits (owner decisions 2026-11-05)

- Rule bound: a lower bound on the commanded duty that depends on the water temperature only, independent of the model and of every other row. Plain config values `min_curve` as `[water C, duty %]` per channel, piecewise linear, constant beyond the end points, validated to rise (strictly rising water, non-falling duty, 0..100). Fan default `[[35, 20], [38, 54.4], [42, 100]]` (54.4 % is 800 rpm on the default calibration), below `fan_start_water` the bound is 0 and the zero-rpm rule applies. Pump default `[[35, 15], [36, 100]]`. The pump above 36 C at 100 % is the owner's choice for a floor of 15 %; there are no data at the floor above 36 C.
- It is enforced every tick before the planner (`enforce_bound`), so a missing forecast, an unwarm model or a missing CPU or GPU cannot stop it. A missing fan or pump rpm row does not stop the bound write either, but the channel then hands over to the firmware through the follow check: no rpm row counts as not following, and the fallback comes after `follow_secs` (60 s). The bound overrides the rate limit upward and is added to the shadow target and shown target. A shortfall below the bound smaller than `deadband_duty` is tolerated and bound writes are at least 5 s apart, except leaving 0. A failed forecast (planner error), a missing model, stale water or the water limit hand the channel to the firmware PID. An unwarm model no longer does that while running (activation still needs a warm model): the plan is held and only the bound moves the command.
- Bound latch and daily budget: the bound that is enforced is a latch on the curve value, not the curve itself. A rise of the curve value is taken at once (never delayed) and set above the curve by `bound_raise_step` (8 duty points, only above the channel floor and only when the channel is already running), but by no more than the rise of the curve value within the last 600 s, so one write covers a whole climb while a steady load gets no lift (the unlimited lift made the pump swing 15 to 100 % up to 32 times a day at constant 42.5 to 57.5 W on the measured and 70 to 80 W on the alternative plant); a raise write is exempt from the daily budget while fewer than `bound_raise_cap` (120, at least 1) exempt raises happened in the last hour (a guard against a runaway sensor; beyond it the write counts against the budget and can be refused). Only raises are exempt: a planner write that lowers the duty, including one that lowers it down to the bound, always counts against the budget. The latch goes down only when the water has been at least `bound_drop_k` (1 K) below the highest water seen since it was set for `bound_hold_secs` (900 s) without a break; it then goes to the highest curve value of that window, not to the instantaneous one, and the peak water is kept, so the next step down needs only another hold and no further fall (before, a drop reset the peak to the cold reading and at equilibrium the next drop never came: after one load fan and pump stayed high for good). Chatter back-off (pump only): the lift limit alone is not enough away from the two sampled plants. With the measured plant at ambient 20 to 24 C or half the heat capacity and a constant 45 to 90 W the pump still swung up to 49 times (742 writes) a day, because the equilibrium at the floor sits just above the 35 C slope: the water climbs, the pump goes to 100 %, the water falls 1.1 K, the hold ends, the pump drops, and the cycle repeats every 1 to 2 h. Now a release followed by a new rise of the curve within 3 h counts as chatter and doubles the fall `bound_drop_k` needed for the next release (twice at most, so 4 K), until a release happens after 12 h without chatter (the required fall is only relaxed at a release, so at a constant load, where the water never falls 4 K, the pump stays raised until the load drops; r13 already shows the pump at 90 % or more for 94 to 100 % of the day at constant loads). The fan has no back-off (its `min_run_secs` already limits its cycling, and a back-off kept the fan on below 33 C for 68 % of the day on the recurring profile against the 30 % goal). The cost is that after a chatter the pump can stay raised until the water falls 4 K below its peak. A latch therefore needs about two holds to reach the floor after a load, because the first drop only gets down to the highest curve value of the descent. Falling duty is written in coarse steps (`down_step_duty`, 35 duty points, per channel; 0 gives the old `step_down_rpm` ramp of about 7 to 10 points): the rate-limited down ramp of the pump was the largest single source of writes (about 15 per release). Falling needs no extra protection, the bound still enforces every raise. Before this change every planner write with duty at or below the bound and every bound write was exempt, so the budget did not exist while the bound was binding (pump slope 85 %/K with a 2 % deadband is 0.024 K, 4261 writes per 24 h at 35.50 +-0.05 C). The cost is that the fan or pump stays raised for up to `bound_hold_secs` after a load peak. A step test runs on `step_duty`, the larger of the planned step and the bound: it stays flagged as overridden for as long as the latch holds a peak, so a step test is only valid from a cold start (water under `fan_start_water`, latch back at the floor, about two holds after the last load); that is a requirement, not a defect.
- Minimum run time (M4): once restarted (or leaving shadow stop) the fan may not go back to 0 for `min_run_secs` (fan default 600 s, near the 8 min time constant; pump 0). A restart is never delayed.
- Frozen water (M5): four rules, each counts as frozen on its own. (a) Plain: the water reading is exactly unchanged for 600 s at any load and any duty, no CPU condition. It used to be a band of 0.01 K (one sensor step), but a reading that flickers between two adjacent steps is what a healthy sensor does at a true steady state: with the measured sensor noise before rounding (Gaussian, sd 0.003 K, 66 % of consecutive samples unchanged) the old rule fired 442 times in 66 constant-load days (30 to 400 W, both plants, three heat capacities) while the exact rule fired 0 times (longest exactly flat run in those days 152 s). ## Restart without the 30 min warm-up (2026-11-05)

- Why the model was recomputed after every restart: the feature engine keeps EWMA state of up to 1800 s (and derivative lags up to 900 s) in memory, `XgbModel::observe` resets it after a gap of more than 10 s, and nothing was stored, so every restart, crash or watchdog relaunch started with empty state and the model counted as warm only after 30 min of complete features.
- Now: on start the daemon reads the telemetry of the last `seed_secs` (default 7200) from the hot store, before the committer can touch it, and replays it through the model second by second (`XgbModel::seed`). `HotStore` keeps a copy of the newest six sealed telemetry files (files seal hourly, and a clean stop seals a short one, so six keep the 7200 s of seed_secs even after a few quick restarts) in `hot/tail` (taken at every rotation and when leftovers are sealed at start), because the committer deletes sealed files after the commit and a clean stop commits everything.
- Only when the newest stored second is at most `seed_max_gap_secs` (default 300) old; otherwise the model warms up as before. The gap between the last stored second and the first live second (stop, final commit, watchdog, start up) is filled once by linear interpolation of each channel (`bridge`), later gaps behave as before (reset after 10 s).
- Measured on the 2 h golden window (`cargo test seeding_from_the_hot_store -- --nocapture`, max relative deviation |a-b|/(1+|a|) over the 44 features against a run that was never interrupted): full history and no gap 0 (exact, 1e-9); 3600 s of history and no gap 0.055 right away and 0.038 after 600 s (initialisation of the 1800 s EWMAs, 13 % memory of the first value, 1.8 % with 7200 s); gap of 60 s 0.44 at the first second (`gpu_temp.ewm30.d300`), 0.16 after 120 s, 0.34 after 300 s, 0.037 after 600 s; gap of 299 s 0.85 at the first second, 0.65 after 30 s, 0.15 after 120 s, 0.68 after 300 s, 0.032 after 600 s. The large numbers are the derivative features with 120 to 300 s lags that span the gap (die temperatures and heat moved during the gap and the interpolation cannot know how); everything else is within the EWMA initialisation error. Treat forecasts as degraded for the first 5 min after a restart with a gap, not for 30 min.

## Step test model: linear backbone with explicit fan and pump response (branch finish-model, 2026-11-06, synthetic data only)

- What changed against the tree path above: `python/steptrain.py` no longer relabels tree targets. It fits the grey-box plant (two lumps, fan exponent m, pump coefficient gamma, sink temperature, dead time at the pump floor and at 100 %, linear ambient drift) on the step windows, and carries the result as an explicit term in a linear model: `forecast = backbone(features) - gain * k * max(0, h - dead(pump rpm)) * step.fx` with `step.fx = (fan rpm / 1460)^m * (1 + gamma (pump rpm / 4800 - 1)) * (water - Ta)` and `k = A / c1`. The backbone is a linear model (ridge or LinearSVR, picked on a separate fold) on the physical set without any fan or pump conductance feature, so it cannot learn a fan effect from the 99 % full-fan history; the term is built from the step test. `model_infer` evaluates the term (`response` block of the model file: feature, pump feature, k, pump rpm range, dead times, gain per series; cpu and gpu use gain 1.0 as an assumption, die temperatures follow the coolant) and a per-feature clip exemption (`free`). Python mirror: `LinearExporter.predict`. The Rust golden test `step_model_with_response_term_matches_python_golden` matches to 1e-9.
- Scaling: `Prep` is fit on the rows the model is trained on (fit and select folds, step rows included), not before the low-pump rows are stacked in. The low-pump indicator and its interactions are exempt from the clip of 5; clipped fraction on fresh1, fresh2, held-out step rows and oor_pump20 is at most 0.06 % (was 63 % on the old holdout). Targets: water, cpu and gpu at 30, 60, 90 and 120 s, so the configured horizon always exists.
- Protocol (one for linear and trees): fit on train, oor_pump20, fresh0 and the step rows outside the hold-out; family and parameters selected on val and fresh1; refit on both; final fold test, fresh2 and held-out step rows. The held-out step rows (`step_hold`) are exactly the grey-box hold span of each window (fan from phase 6 on, pump from phase 4 on, up to the end of the segment); `step_train` is every other window row more than the embargo (900 s) before a hold span, so the two blocks cannot overlap (the first merge had 73 % of the held rows also in `step_train`, and the response term was fitted with them; `report.json` now carries `hold_rows` and `overlap_rows`, the latter 0), and the fit and the response term use only rows outside the hold span. Segments of neighbouring windows no longer share their rest rows (each takes half). The depth-3 tree comparison (deployed 44 features, `nthread = 1`) is trained and scored the same way; it is reported, not shipped, because trees cannot carry the fan term.
- Reproducibility: ridge and SVR are deterministic, trees run on one thread, bootstrap seeds are fixed. Measured with the restart-gap case at 30.5 C, seeds 0 to 2, one BLAS and xgboost thread against six: the model files are byte-identical, fit RMSE and fan effect ratio are equal, the reports differ by at most 4e-15 relative. `test_steptrain.py` compares thread settings 1 and 4 of the xgboost threads.
- Gate (also in `report.json` as `criteria`), second version, written after the first real refusal and therefore post hoc, not pre-registered (the thresholds were fixed before the first run of this version, not independently of what the first version showed): (1) fan excitation, (2) full-fan conductance A estimated from the earlier half of the non-step history (steady 60 s blocks above 1400 rpm and 4000 pump rpm, first 300 s of each piece left out, slope against block water temperature of the block heat minus the stored heat c1 dTw/dt + c2 dT2/dt, with lump 2 reconstructed from the measured water as a lag of time constant c2/K; the first version used one capacity 11.7 kJ/K and was +3.5 % biased on the simulated plant, the two-lump version -0.25 %, validated on simulation only) must be 20 to 28 W/K and is then held fixed in the grey-box fit (the no-fan fits keep A free); fit RMSE at most 0.12 K, (3) m positive with a likelihood-ratio bound (standard errors inflated 10 x, i.e. the statistic is divided by 100, variance inflated 100 x) against the model with no fan response (m = 0, A free) but with ambient drift; the two models are not nested because the full fit holds A fixed at the history estimate while the no-fan fit keeps A free, so the statistic is a heuristic bound and not an exact chi-square test, and the conductance at the lowest held running level at least 25 % below full fan, (3b) C within 8 to 16 kJ/K, (4) on fan phases 6 and 7 the 120 s forecast strictly better than persistence and the lower bound of the circular 240 s block-bootstrap ratio of the no-fan refit (C 8 to 16 kJ/K, Ta 18 to 30 C) over the full model above 1.0, (5) final-fold water 30 s and 120 s RMSE not worse than the deployed model's beyond 1.96 times the bootstrap standard deviation, held-out step rows not worse than persistence beyond the same band, (6) on the later half of the non-step history the 120 s forecast at least 1.5 times better than persistence and the absolute mean bias above 1400 rpm at most 0.10 K. The band-derived `fit_rmse_max` and `hold_rmse_max` of the first version were removed. The forecasts in (4) and (6) use the logged power, pump rpm and fan rpm inside the horizon, because the planner compares fan choices under the same load: it is a physics test, not a planner skill score; the variants with power held and with power and pump held are reported and are much weaker on the first real data (no edge over the no-fan reference when power and pump are held). Phases 6 and 7 are at constant full fan, so the fan shape m is supported only by the fit rows, the likelihood-ratio test and the leave-one-phase-out runs; those fits include the hold-out rows. q0 (65 to 84 W, depending on A) has an unknown physical source, the measured idle heat is about 36 W, and it lies on a ridge with Ta, A and m. The first real fit had A 32.3 W/K and shipped; the steady full-fan history gives 23.3 to 23.8 W/K and that fit had no skill over persistence on the history (120 s RMSE 0.399 K against 0.406 K). Second version on the same data: A 23.56 (767 blocks), fit 0.094 K, C 14.2, m 1.09, Ta 21.05, q0 84.1 W, `tau_hi` on its lower bound 3.0 s, forecast on phases 6 and 7 0.210 K against 0.618 K persistence, history 0.238 K against 0.398 K, bias above 1400 rpm +0.1005 K, refused by 0.0005 K on criterion (6); candidate files only, nothing deployed. Third version (two-lump estimator of A, developed and validated on simulation only; the real 0.1005 K refusal was known before this fix and before the dry-run acceptance was met): on the real data A 22.89 (759 blocks), fit 0.0947 K, C 14.5, m 1.08, Ta 20.71, q0 86.0 W, forecast on phases 6 and 7 0.213 K against 0.618 K persistence (ratio 2.24, interval 1.41 to 2.91), history 0.240 K against 0.398 K, bias above 1400 rpm +0.111 K (+0.245, +0.254, +0.135 in the lower bins), refused on criterion (6) alone; candidate files only, nothing deployed, Rust `load_dir` and golden parity pass. Dry run (`docs/steptest.md` section 9): estimator -0.25 % against the plant truth (first version +3.5 %), the correct plant ships in 60 of 60 (first version 1 of 60), the no-fan, drift, bump and `alt_nofan` cases ship in 0 of 80; the wrong plants `wrong_air` ship in 11 of 20, `wrong_recirc12` and `wrong_recirc161` in 0 of 20 each (19 and 2 of 20 would ship without criterion (6)). The pump is not in the gate: `identified.pump` is always `false`; a probe with the plant's pump coefficient at 0, 0.3 and 0.6 fitted 0.14, 0.43 and 0.72 and raised the flag in 0, 0 and 2 of 5 runs, so it carries no information at the plausible size (0.03).
- Runbook dry run (`python steptrain.py dryrun`, `stepsim.py`): the protocol of `docs/steptest.md` sections 3 and 4 as one table in `SIM["inject"]["protocols"]["runbook"]` (a test parses the doc table and plans and compares): rests 900/180/300/300/900 s, fan duty test 0 % 120 s, pump duty test 15 % 120 s, fan test of 7 phases with loads 250/100/75/70/70/250/125 W, pump test with 200 W, the safety curve emulated in the plant (duty = max(step, bound(water)), phase 5 reaches 35.6 C), start water 30 to 31 C (the runbook band), optional restart gap (all channels missing for 120 s, ending 30 s before the fan test, as a clean restart leaves it). Why the lead is 250 W: with the doc table of the first merge (120 W lead) the held-out phase 6 at 250 W was a load extrapolation (fit loads 70 to 120 W; 4 seeds each, hold-out limit 0.15 K at the time: 4 of 4 shipped at 30 C, 1 of 4 at 30.5 C, 0 of 4 at 31 C, hold-out RMSE 0.07 to 0.29 K) and the review's passes at 30 to 31 C had the 250 W rows of the pump windows, which overlapped the held-out rows, in the fit.
