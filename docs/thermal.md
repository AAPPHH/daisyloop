Generated by `python/inertia.py` (stages water, dynamics, settings, tables, ext, steptest, physical, bootstrap, report; `inertia_ext.py` holds the two-lump, event, frozen-power and restart analyses, `inertia_report.py` this text). Read-only analysis of the lake extract (Parquet copy); nothing was written to hardware, no device was opened, `src/` is unchanged. Every number below was measured or simulated by that script unless marked ESTIMATE. This version applies the corrections of the independent review (H1 to H3, M1 to M7, LOW).

Owner's limits used throughout: water plan limit 46 C, firmware takeover above 48 C, top limit 50 C; CPU and GPU temperatures never trigger anything; pump floor 15 %; fan stop 33 C, restart 35 C, start duty 20 %; decision 30 s, horizon 240 s.

## 2. Water model

C dTw/dt = eta_cpu Pcpu + eta_gpu Pgpu + Ppump + P0 - G(fan, pump) (Tw - Ta), with G = Gn + A (rpm/1460)^m (1 + gamma (pump_rpm/4800 - 1)). Ppump is the measured D5 pump power, P0 is zero in the headline fit and free in the ambient profile. Fitted by multi-step output error on 30 min windows (30 s steps, exact exponential step, Levenberg-Marquardt). Only eta/G and eta/C are identified in absolute terms (the review finds eta about 0.8 and G 18.6 W/K if eta is free), so absolute C and G assume eta near 1; predictions in measured watts are not affected.

### 2.4 Physical plausibility

- C against an estimate (ESTIMATE, my assumptions): coolant 1.5 to 2.5 L at 4.0 to 4.18 kJ/(L K), three radiators at 0.4 to 1.0 kJ/K metal each, blocks, pump and fittings 0.4 to 0.8 kJ/K: 7.6 to 14.2 kJ/K. The fitted 11.7 kJ/K lies inside; this check is weak because the range is wide.
- Heat share: eta_cpu = 0.96 is physical. eta_gpu is pushed to the cap 1.0 (unconstrained it goes to 1.1); fixing it lower costs SSE 7984 at 0.8 and 6601 at 0.9 against 6338, so the GPU block delivers at least about 0.9 of the NVML board power. CPU and GPU heat shares agree at a ratio of 0.98 [0.86, 1.10] in the review's slope-corrected 10 min blocks (weakly resolved).
- G at full fan about 24 W/K for three 360 radiators is the right order (ESTIMATE of a typical 360 radiator at full fan: 6 to 10 W/K each).

### 3.4 Heat soak after load ends

17 events (10 min earlier mean 269 W CPU+GPU falling to 133 W). Mean water change after the drop (observed / one-lump model, K): 30s -0.13/-0.19, 60s -0.60/-0.52, 120s -1.36/-1.08, 300s -1.84/-1.69, 600s -1.81/-1.81. ## 4. Fan and pump: what the data can and cannot identify

### 4.1 Pump: confounded with the water temperature

- Pump below 2800 rpm ran for 15.1 h with a maximum water temperature of 35.76 C: 0.26 h above 35 C and 0.00 h above 36 C. **The floor pump above 36 C has zero data**; everything from 36 to 50 C at the floor is extrapolated. ### 4.5 Sensor quality and fan response

- Resolution: 0.01 K per step. 68 % of consecutive 1 s samples are unchanged and 27 % differ by exactly one step. Smallest resolvable rate: 0.02 K/min over 30 s, 0.005 K/min over 120 s.
- Noise on quiet minutes (599 blocks): residual SD about a straight line 5.4 mK median; quantisation alone would be 2.9 mK, so the sensor is quantisation limited.
- Two paths to the same sensor (D5 report and LHM): difference 3.4 mK mean, SD 27 mK, step correlation 0.86 at lag 0.
- Lag of the sensor against the true water is not separable from transit (3.1).
- Fan spool: on 12 duty jumps the rpm reaches 63 % after 4 s (p90 6 s) and 90 % after 9 s (p90 14 s), i.e. t63 4 to 5 s, t90 8 to 12 s; the earlier 'below 1 s' came from a grid minimum on data that is 86 % saturated. Spin-up from standstill was never observed: the lowest rpm seen is 138 (held at 144 rpm on 5.2 % duty), and every jump above started from 646 rpm or more. The model uses a first-order spool of 4.5 s. One tach for nine fans: 8 of 9 are unobserved. Static map duty to rpm: 5 % = 144, 33 % = 606, 42 % = 735, 59 % = 999, 68 % = 1117, 79 % = 1238, 87 % = 1324, 100 % = 1460.
- Fan to sensor delay was not measured separately: 'invisible for the dead time' is the model, with one favourable observation (test 1 turned downward within 15 s of the fan restore, n = 1).

### 6.5 Hysteresis against on-off cycling

Idle heat is 40 to 120 W. With the fan stopped the water climbs through the 2 K band in 11 min at 40 W and 3 min at 120 W (one lump, G_stop 0.5). The binding fact is the running side: at the start duty (389 rpm) G = 2.9 W/K (alt 7.6) removes 26 W at 34 C (alt 69 W). Rpm needed to hold 34 C at idle heat of 40 / 60 / 80 / 120 W: 40.0 W: 510 rpm, 60.0 W: 650 rpm, 80.0 W: 780 rpm, 120.0 W: 1000 rpm (alt: 40.0 W: 150, 60.0 W: 240, 80.0 W: 340, 120.0 W: 530). So at the start duty (near-stopped, 4.2) the water does not come back to the stop temperature at idle loads above ~30 to 90 W; a stop-restart cycle exists only if the controller raises the duty. ### 6.6 Rate limits (current +700 / -150 rpm per decision, pump +1000 / -200)

Up: 700 rpm per 30 s reaches full speed from stop in 90 s; with a command at 120 s the fan is full at 184 s (limited) against 138 s (free, spool 4.5 s), the water is 38.2 against 37.3 C at that moment and the steady state is the same. The limit costs about 0.9 K at 400 W and protects acoustics and the unmodelled start-up of nine fans. Down: 150 rpm per 30 s needs 240 s from full to the start duty, half of the slow tau (a 400 W load that returns inside that window finds the cooling still there); this is the safe direction. The pump: its effect on G is below 10 % and its dead time is the larger effect (31 s at 20 % against 11 s at 100 %), the pump rate limits are not constrained by the data (a pump change only shifts the transit time and costs a 0.2 to 0.35 K transient at the sensor); keep them.

### 6.7 Smoothing (cpu_smooth_secs = 30)

`cpu_smooth_secs` smooths only the CPU temperature used for the plan bound (`control.rs:472`, `plan_temps`); it does not smooth power and there is no GPU power smoothing knob. The heat reaching the water is not delayed by it, and the water forecast sees the true power. What it can do is hold a die temperature spike below a bound: the raw core-max reaches 104 C, and the exponential averages reach 104.0 / 103.9 / 103.6 C at 30 / 60 / 120 s and never touch 105 C (0 s at the limit), so at 60 s the knob does nothing more than at 30 s. CPU and GPU temperatures never trigger anything (owner's rule). For power spikes (a 5 s spike of +200 W is 4 to 13 sensor steps, 3.3) the filter fractions are:

| tau_s | 5 s | 30 s | 2 min | 10 min |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 10 | 39 % | 95 % | 100 % | 100 % |
| 30 | 15 % | 63 % | 98 % | 100 % |
| 60 | 8 % | 39 % | 86 % | 100 % |
| 120 | 4 % | 22 % | 63 % | 99 % |

**Keep cpu_smooth_secs at 30.**

### 6.8 Planning limit margin (46 C planning, 48 C takeover)

Frozen-power forecast error out-of-sample (2.1): RMSE 0.73 / 0.90 / 1.28 K at 240 / 300 / 480 s, so 2 sigma is 1.5 to 2.6 K and the p99 under-prediction 2.0 to 3.5 K (max 4.8 to 4.9 K). Reaction latency at the highest heating rate (400 W, stopped fan, 2.8 K/min over dead time + one tick, about 1 min) adds about 3 K. Needed margin about 1.5 to 2.6 K (2 sigma) up to 5.4 K with latency; the 2 K between 46 and 48 C does not cover it on its own. What covers it today is the effective plan limit of about 43.5 C (46 C minus weight x span), which leaves 2.5 K to 46 C. **Keep the weight shift active.** If the owner wants a true 46 C, accept 1.5 to 2.6 K of forecast error against the 2 K gap. Do not raise the planning limit towards 48 C: at 500 W the full-fan steady state is already 45.3 C (sink 25 C) and the capacity limits of section 5 apply.

### 6.9 Does heat soak matter

No (3.4): after a load ends the water follows the model within 0.3 K with no continued rise beyond the transit. After a 300 W load ends the decay has the slow tau, 8 min at full fan, and the fan can step down on the forecast without a soak buffer.

## 8. Limits of this analysis

- Two lumps with dead time are fitted on 39 + 15 events; the split (C1, K) is resolved only to about +-40 % and C1 can be as low as 4 kJ/K, so every short-term number carries that, and the C/2 case bounds it.
- The frozen-power study uses the measured fan and pump trajectory as the plan; a planner that chooses the fan would differ. The sink is unknown; the capacity table is an extrapolation in the sink.
- The short-spike table is simulated, not observed.
- G below full fan, G with the fan stopped, the pump above 36 C at the floor, and the dead time at the 15 % pump floor are extrapolations or estimates, marked as such.
- Fan swap to iCUE LINK fans changes G(rpm) and the spool: all fan numbers are for the current nine fans with one tach.
- The Parquet copy ends 2026-11-05 ~07:00 UTC; four days, 52.6 h with CPU power, no summer sink.
- Ray: `ray status` is broken by a click traceback here and no Ray cluster was running. The bootstrap (120 draws) and the step-test Monte-Carlo (6 tasks of 1 CPU, 6 of 24 cores, the machine was already loaded by another job) each ran on one local Ray cluster started by the run and shut down at its end (ray, local cluster started by this run and shut down afterwards, 6 tasks of 1 CPU). The interpreter was the Python of the `other_project` venv (Ray 2.54.1, as `docs/model.md` did) because the conda Python's Ray lacks the dashboard dependencies; a first start with it failed and left its own `gcs_server` (PID 48004), which I stopped, as I did the stale serial bootstrap of the previous worker (PID 35964).
